# SkyGuard roof-replacement page: primary-source research

Verified September 18, 2026. Read-only research; no submissions, purchases, outreach or site edits. Recommendations below are editorial judgment, not a ranking forecast.

## 1. Fort Worth permits and decking: useful, specific answers

**Publish a direct permit FAQ:** Within Fort Worth, replacing roof decking, lathing/sheathing boards, rafters or ridge boards requires a building permit. Shingle-only work does not require one; shingle-layer restrictions still apply. The City explicitly confirms that replacing **any** decking triggers a permit. Do not say all roof replacements require permits or that all reroofing is permit-exempt. Confirm the property jurisdiction and final scope before work; nearby cities have different requirements.

Sources: [City residential permitting](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/departments/development-services/permits/residential-information), [City roofing FAQ, questions 6–7](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/files/assets/public/v/2/development-services/documents/resources-applications-forms-videos/r/roofing-faq.pdf).

**Explain decking inspection without overselling replacement:** The City's FAQ says sound sheathing compliant when originally installed does not automatically need complete replacement merely because codes changed. It does not allow a second decking layer over the existing layer. Underlayment must meet the more restrictive applicable manufacturer instructions or IRC requirements. Its reroofing answers also address drip edges and crickets; exact roof details still govern scope. Avoid a universal thickness mandate, blanket full redecking requirement, or guaranteed insurance payment for decking.

Source: [City roofing FAQ, questions 2–5 and 8–12](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/files/assets/public/v/2/development-services/documents/resources-applications-forms-videos/r/roofing-faq.pdf).

**Recommended estimate detail:** Identify existing deck condition, concealed-damage uncertainty, the approval process and unit-price basis for extra decking, permit responsibility, flashing/drip-edge details, underlayment and cleanup. These are proposed customer decision aids, not claims that a specific SkyGuard contract already includes them.

## 2. Ventilation: explain the assessment, avoid generic promises

The City links its adopted **2021 IRC with local amendments**. The publisher's model-code R806.2 generally calls for net free ventilation area of 1/150 of vented attic area, with a conditional 1/300 exception and upper/lower vent-distribution requirements. This is **not** a universal instruction to add ridge vents or a verified Fort Worth-specific numerical amendment interpretation. The City's linked large amended-code PDF did not load in this bounded web check. Do not publish the numeric ratio as an unconditional local rule.

Safe public wording: “A replacement estimate should evaluate the attic's ventilation design, available intake and exhaust, and the proposed roofing manufacturer's requirements. The right approach depends on whether the attic is vented or uses a designed unvented assembly.” Avoid promised utility savings or suggesting that more exhaust alone fixes ventilation.

Sources: [Fort Worth adopted codes](https://www.fortworthtexas.gov/departments/development-services/permits/building-energy-codes), [ICC 2021 IRC R806.2](https://codes.iccsafe.org/s/IRC2021P3/chapter-8-roof-ceiling-construction/IRC2021P3-Pt03-Ch08-SecR806.2). Also see [ICC R806.3–R806.5 ventilation, clearance and unvented assemblies](https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/IRC2021P2/chapter-8-roof-ceiling-construction). Verify assembly-specific application during project design.

## 3. Class 3 versus Class 4: buyer-friendly comparison

| Topic | Accurate explanation |
|---|---|
| Class 3 | UL 2218 laboratory impact classification; GAF's official technical explanation describes a 1.75-inch steel ball dropped from 17 feet. |
| Class 4 | Highest UL 2218 class; the corresponding test uses a 2-inch steel ball from 20 feet. |
| What it does not establish | Neither class guarantees no hail damage, a particular roof lifespan, insurance coverage or an automatic discount. A steel-ball diameter is not a guaranteed safe hailstone size. |
| What the buyer should receive | Exact proposed product and classification documentation, complete installation scope, applicable warranty and any insurer-required paperwork. |

The test dimensions above were verified in the manufacturer's explanation, not a purchased full UL standard. UL's own public standard scope establishes that the test assesses specified steel-ball impacts; it excludes aging/weathering effects and does not establish a direct hailstone-to-steel-ball performance correlation. Thus Class 4 is a higher laboratory category, not a promise of hail immunity.

Sources: [UL 2218 official scope, May 2024 revision](https://www.shopulstandards.com/ProductDetail.aspx?productId=UL2218), [GAF official impact-testing explanation](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/plan-design/impact-resistant-roof).

TDI says insurers decide accepted test criteria, documentation and discount amounts. Tell customers to ask their insurer about the **exact** proposed product before choosing an upgrade. Do not advertise a savings percentage. TDI's PC068 discussion specifically distinguishes TWIA requirements from other insurers' choices.

Source: [Texas Department of Insurance roofing credits](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/company/roofing-discounts.html).

## 4. Warranties: distinguish material, enhanced-system and workmanship coverage

**TAMKO:** The current Edge comparison allows Gold contractors to offer the standard limited warranty and qualifying TAMKO Shield Enhanced Limited System Warranty. Shield explicitly has **no workmanship coverage** and has accessory/product requirements. Higher program tiers have different options. Do not imply SkyGuard's Pro Gold status grants every TAMKO warranty or a universal manufacturer-backed workmanship term. Current product-specific HailGuard provisions also differ; avoid treating all TAMKO shingles identically. Source: [TAMKO Edge official comparison](https://www.tamko.com/edge).

**CertainTeed:** Its official explanation separates contractor workmanship promises from manufacturer defect coverage. Standard SureStart provides initial non-prorated manufacturing-defect protection for the applicable product period. Enhanced SureStart PLUS eligibility depends on the contractor tier, system and selected coverage; its current educational page assigns 4/5-star workmanship options to PRO/PREMIER, not merely base ShingleMaster. SkyGuard's owner-confirmed credential is base ShingleMaster. Avoid borrowing higher-tier coverage or universal numeric terms. Official pages contain differing historical numeric workmanship summaries, strengthening the case to link the actual issued warranty rather than publish a blanket number. Sources: [CertainTeed warranty basics](https://www.certainteed.com/roofing-101-worry-free-warranties), [current credential program](https://www.certainteed.com/roofing-education-credential-us), [coverage comparison](https://www.certainteed.com/inspiration/how-tos/why-credentialing-matters).

**Recommended page module:** Explain these three coverage categories in three short rows. Tell homeowners to review product name, coverage period, exclusions, registration, transfer terms and who handles a claim in the written proposal. Link the credentials page. Do not invent SkyGuard workmanship duration.

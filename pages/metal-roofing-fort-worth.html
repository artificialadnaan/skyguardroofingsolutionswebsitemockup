Stone-coated steel or standing seam?

Stone-coated steel: textured shake, tile or shingle profiles

These formed panels combine steel with a stone-granule surface. TEK’s range includes shake, tile and shingle profiles; compare samples and the exact product documentation in the manufacturer’s technical resources. This option is worth discussing when you want a textured roof appearance and need to evaluate a specific panel assembly for your home.

Standing seam: raised seams and continuous roof lines

Standing-seam systems typically use raised panel seams and concealed attachment. Some use clips that accommodate panel movement. The details vary by system, including seam type and slope requirements; see McElroy Metal’s standing-seam system overview for manufacturer examples. Use product-specific specifications to compare fastening details and confirm the exact system proposed for your roof.

Choose by the actual design, installation scope and written terms. Neither the word “metal” nor a particular appearance establishes a universal hail rating, energy saving or service life.

What the roof assessment should resolve

  • Slope and drainage: match each roof section to the proposed system’s requirements, including valleys and transitions.
  • Deck and preparation: identify the substrate condition, tear-off approach, underlayment and allowances for repairs.
  • Attachment and movement: specify the product’s fastening or clip details rather than assuming one method fits every panel.
  • Edges and penetrations: include compatible flashing around walls, chimneys, vents and other roof openings.
  • Finish and care: review coating information, cleaning guidance, maintenance access and any warranty exclusions.

A complicated roof may require different details from a simple roof plane. Review those details in the estimate instead of comparing only the headline price. Our roof replacement planning page covers preparation, scheduling and written expectations.

Metal roofing questions

Which metal roof is best for my home?

There is no single best system for every property. Compare roof geometry, appearance, preparation, installation details and budget. An inspection helps establish whether your preferred system fits the roof before you commit to a quote.

Will a metal roof lower my energy bills?

Do not assume savings from the material name alone. Ask for evidence for the selected product and assembly, then consider your home’s insulation, ventilation and existing conditions. A product feature is not a guaranteed household saving.

What should the proposal say about warranties?

It should identify the exact product and applicable written terms, plus any separate workmanship coverage. Review exclusions, maintenance, registration and transfer conditions. Warranty length alone is not a reliable way to compare complete roof systems.

Roof Damage Documentation and Insurance Questions for Southlake, TX Homeowners

Published April 20, 2026 — By SkyGuard Roofing Solutions

Editorial review: September 8, 2026

If your Southlake roof may have storm damage, start with a safe inspection and a clear record of what happened. The roofing scope and the insurance coverage decision are related, but they are handled by different parties.

Prepare your information

Keep your policy number, insurer’s claims contact, storm date, and any earlier roof records together. Photograph visible exterior damage from the ground and interior stains only where access is safe. Do not postpone contacting the insurer while waiting for a complete photo package or a contractor appointment. Ask the insurer for the deadlines that apply to your policy and claim.

Ask about your specific coverage

Have your insurer explain the deductible, applicable limits and exclusions, roof settlement provisions, and whether the claim is evaluated using actual cash value or replacement cost. Replacement-cost coverage is not a promise that every invoice item will be paid. Ask what documentation and completion deadlines apply to any recoverable depreciation. Keep the response with your claim records.

Understand the roofing estimate

A construction estimate should identify measured roof areas, products, tear-off, disposal, flashing details, and known repair needs. Ask how concealed decking damage would be priced and approved. Estimating software organizes quantities and costs; its name alone does not establish damage, coverage, or the amount your insurer owes. Compare the proposed construction scope with the observations in the roof report.

SkyGuard’s role

SkyGuard can inspect the roof, photograph observations, prepare a construction estimate, and explain the proposed roofing work. The homeowner handles the claim with the insurer. We do not file or negotiate claims for homeowners, represent them on coverage questions, or promise a settlement. Texas also prohibits roofing contractors from waiving, rebating, or absorbing an insurance deductible.

Keep the claim and project records aligned

If additional construction work is discovered, request photographs and a written change description before authorizing it. Share those records with your insurer and ask how it wants additional documentation submitted. Do not assume a contractor’s change order changes insurance coverage. At completion, keep invoices, payment records, warranty documents, and the final scope; ask your insurer what it still needs.

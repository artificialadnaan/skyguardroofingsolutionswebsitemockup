Roof Inspection Checklist for Weatherford, TX Homeowners

Published April 6, 2026 — By SkyGuard Roofing Solutions

Editorial review: September 8, 2026

This Weatherford checklist helps you organize ground-level observations before a professional inspection. It is not a substitute for roof access, structural assessment, or leak diagnosis by a qualified professional.

Record exterior observations safely

From a stable ground-level position, note missing or displaced roofing, changes along the ridge, bent flashing, and debris. Photograph the whole roof area before any closer visible detail so the location is understandable. Do not climb a ladder to see hidden sections. If branches, utility lines, or damaged structures create a hazard, keep clear and seek the appropriate help.

Check the roof edge and drainage

Look for visibly sagging gutters, displaced downspouts, staining on fascia or soffits, and signs that runoff collects near the house. These are observations to investigate, not proof of a particular defect. Gutter cleaning and roof-edge repairs need a safe access plan. The drainage solution should fit the roof area and site rather than a universal downspout-extension distance.

Note interior symptoms

In safe, normally accessible rooms, record water stains, peeling finishes, or active dripping and when they occur. Stains can come from roofing, plumbing, or condensation and may remain after an old leak was repaired. Do not step into unboarded attic areas, touch suspect wiring, or enter a hot or damaged attic to investigate. Tell the inspector if the ceiling or roofline appears unstable.

Bring the right records

Collect the approximate installation date, material information, warranty documents, and earlier repair invoices. Mark which leaks recur at the same location. Previous photographs can help compare changes. If the inspection concerns storm damage, keep the storm date distinct from the date damage was discovered and ask the insurer directly about reporting requirements.

Ask what the inspection can establish

A useful report identifies inspected areas, photographs, findings, recommended work, and access limits. Concealed underlayment, framing, and decking cannot always be evaluated without further investigation. A noninvasive inspection is not a guarantee against future leaks. Ask whether a structural, electrical, plumbing, or energy specialist is needed for issues outside roofing scope.

Turn findings into a work plan

Ask which items need prompt attention and which can be monitored. Compare a localized repair with replacement when both are viable. Keep reports and completed-work records for future maintenance and any insurer questions.

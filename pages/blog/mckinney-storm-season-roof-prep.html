Storm Season Roof Preparation Tips for McKinney, TX Homeowners

Prepare a McKinney home for severe weather by keeping a condition record, addressing known defects, and organizing contacts before a storm.

Published 2026-03-16 · By SkyGuard Roofing Solutions · Editorial review: September 8, 2026

Prepare a McKinney home for severe weather by keeping a condition record, addressing known defects, and organizing contacts before a storm. Preparation reduces avoidable uncertainty; it does not make a roof immune to hail or wind.

Review the roof before problems become urgent

Gather the installation date, prior repair invoices, and any recurring leak locations. If the condition is uncertain, request a professional assessment. Ask which roof areas were examined and what needs repair or monitoring. Roof-level access and concealed materials have inspection limits, so a report should explain what was not verified as well as what was observed.

Arrange drainage and tree maintenance

Have gutter and downspout function checked and ask a tree professional about dead or overhanging limbs. Homeowners can note visible obstructions from ground level without climbing. The appropriate discharge route depends on the site; do not direct runoff toward neighboring structures. Keep receipts and photos of any maintenance so your records show what was done and when.

Ask about flashings and roof transitions

During an inspection, request attention to visible conditions at chimneys, skylights, vents, valleys, and roof-to-wall connections. Flashing must work with the roof system; adding caulk is not always a durable repair. Do not access the eaves by ladder to inspect or apply sealant yourself. Have the contractor explain the cause and scope of any proposed correction.

Prepare household information

Keep the insurer’s claims number, policy details, a roofing contact, and photos of the home in a place you can access after an outage. Follow National Weather Service alerts and identify your household’s shelter plan. Do not wait for the roof inspection to prepare for personal safety, and do not go outside to check the roof during severe weather.

After the storm

Wait for safe conditions and stay away from fallen lines, unstable trees, and damaged areas. Photograph visible changes from the ground and report active interior leaks. Contact your insurer promptly for its requirements; do not delay notification while arranging a roofing visit. Weather data can supply context, but only a property assessment can address your roof’s condition.

Schedule with current information

Tell SkyGuard if water is entering or roofing is exposed when requesting a McKinney inspection. Ask about current availability and any suitable temporary protection. Following widespread storms, weather, access, and demand can affect scheduling. Inspection findings and construction estimates document roofing work; they do not establish an insurance settlement.
